Identify 3 Real Businesses

Exercise 1 / 4 . Level 1-3 . 40 min

The instruction was to find three real businesses -- not categories, real places with real people in them -- and map each one against five questions: who is the business, what is the pain, what is the evidence that the pain is real, what would I build, and what is the budget question. Level 1 is just the map. Level 2 adds what tools already exist for this problem and roughly what they charge. Level 3 picks the strongest and outlines a specific solution. I went through all three levels. The three businesses are kept anonymous by policy and by preference -- I am using roles and first names only.

Idea A -- Reentry Case-Management Reminders

DimensionDetail
The businessA small reentry / transitional-housing nonprofit. Two case managers, roughly 30 residents at any given time. Anonymous; I came through a program like this one, so this is not a guess about a world I read about.
The painEvery resident's parole check-ins, court dates, job applications, and certification deadlines live in one shared Google Sheet. Reminders go out the night before by hand -- the case manager, I'll call her Renee, texts each resident individually. That is 6-8 hours a week of manual work, plus a constant low-level fear of a dropped date. A missed parole check-in is not an inconvenience. It can be a violation. A violation can mean going back inside.
The evidenceLived experience. I have watched this happen. It is the same operational gap that ReEntry Tech was gesturing at from the resident side -- this is the staff side of the same problem.
The solutionIntake form -> AI extracts structured fields (name, dates, deadlines) from the messy free-text intake note -> clean record in the sheet -> automated SMS reminder the day before each event + a Monday digest of the week's schedule to the case manager.
The budget questionGrant-funded. Real but tight. The budget right now is being paid in Renee's time and burnout and the occasional dropped date -- it is not a line item labeled "software." The value of a prevented violation is enormous. The money is the genuine constraint, not the willingness to solve the problem.

On pricing uncertainty

The lecture was explicit that ChatGPT hallucinates pricing for nonprofit software and that opaque pricing is itself a finding. Where I could not confirm a real price from a published page, I said so. The Bonterra/Apricot opacity is actually load-bearing: it signals enterprise positioning, which means "not for you" to a two-person nonprofit. That is useful market information even without a number.

Idea B -- Solo 3D-Scan / Drone-Survey Operator Post-Job Admin

DimensionDetail
The businessA solo operator running LiDAR scan jobs and drone surveys -- the kind of independent contractor who bills $1,000 to $3,000 per job and works alone or with one part-time assistant.
The painAfter every scan job: write a plain-language client summary from raw scan statistics, generate an invoice, and send a follow-up email a week out. Three to four hours of admin per job. The follow-ups especially slip -- and that is where repeat work gets lost.
The evidenceI do point-cloud processing work on the side and I know the tools and workflow. The admin end of a scan job is always the ugly part that nobody wants to talk about because it is not the interesting part.
The solutionA "job done" trigger (a form the operator fills after wrapping up) -> AI drafts a plain-language summary from scan stats + a template -> a draft invoice in their accounting tool -> a scheduled follow-up email queued for one week out.
The budget questionA solo operator billing $1,000-$3,000/job can easily justify a few hundred dollars to reclaim a half-day per job. The budget case is strong. But my network into this specific client type is thin -- I am not the operator's warm contact.

Idea C -- Neighborhood Barbershop Online Booking

DimensionDetail
The businessA neighborhood barbershop -- phone only, paper appointment book, one owner-operator.
The painOwner stops mid-cut to answer the phone and pencil people in. No-shows because there are no reminders.
The evidenceEvery barber I have ever talked to has this problem.
The solutionOnline booking form -> calendar -> confirmation text + reminder text the day before.
The budget questionBarbers are not flush with margin. But the real problem is not budget.

Level 2 -- What Tools Already Exist

ToolPriceWhat it does
BooksyFree to ~$30/monthBuilt specifically for barbers: online booking, reminders, client management. Handles exactly this problem.
Square AppointmentsFree for individualsBooking, confirmation texts, reminders, calendar sync. Also handles exactly this problem.
Vagaro, StyleSeat, others$25-30/month rangeSame category. The market is saturated at low price points.
Why Idea C is a stress-test casualty

There is no market gap here. Booksy and Square solve this specific problem for roughly $0-30/month and they are already in barbershops. The pain is real and the problem is solvable -- it just got solved years ago by people with more resources than me. Building this would be competing on price with a free product, which is not a position. A good idea has to fail the competitor-research check sometimes; this is that.

Level 3 -- Picking the Strongest: Idea A

Idea A is the strongest for three reasons that compound: the pain has genuine stakes (a dropped date is not just inconvenient), the existing tools either do not fit (too enterprise) or do not solve it (generic schedulers lack the intake + AI extraction piece), and I have lived context and a warm contact. Idea B is a more lucrative market but I am not the right person to walk in that door without a longer relationship. Idea C has no door to walk through.

The specific solution for Idea A:

  1. An intake form (Google Form or Typeform) where the case manager pastes or types the resident's intake note.
  2. An AI step (Zapier's "AI by Zapier" or an OpenAI API call) that reads the free-text note and extracts: resident name, parole check-in date, court dates, cert deadlines, job application deadlines.
  3. A Google Sheets row is created / updated with those structured fields.
  4. A Twilio SMS step fires a reminder to the resident the day before each extracted date.
  5. A Monday morning digest email (or text) to Renee listing the week's events across all residents.

Tools: Google Forms, Zapier (or a lightweight Cloudflare Worker if the automation platform is too limiting), OpenAI for the extraction step, Google Sheets for the record, Twilio for SMS. This is all plumbing I have laid before in different configurations. Honest time estimate: around eight hours to build and test, assuming clean data in the intake notes, which it will not be, so add two more for the messy cases.

Deliverable

Three businesses mapped against the full framework, with competitor research and honest uncertainty about pricing. One clear casualty (Idea C, displaced by Booksy). One strong candidate parked for later (Idea B, lucrative but thin network). One leading idea with lived credibility, a warm contact, a real gap in the market, and a concrete solution outline (Idea A). Taking that into Exercise 2 for stress-testing.
