Stress-Test Each Other's Ideas

Exercise 2 / 4 . Level 1-2 . 30 min

Breakout rooms. My partner for this exercise went through each of my three ideas with four questions, and I went through his. The four questions are the same for every idea: Is this real? Is it painful enough that someone changes behavior? Can you build it in a week? What would you charge -- and is that a number the client would pay? The instructions said to give honest feedback, not supportive feedback. My partner did. I am grateful for that.

My policy throughout: I am keeping businesses and people at the role level. No org names, no last names, nothing I would not be fine having indexed. That is my call and the program is fine with it.

Yes. 3-4 hours of admin per job at $1-3k/job is a real cost. The slipping follow-ups translate directly to lost repeat work. Yes, but it is a mild pain. The barber is annoyed, not at risk. Feedback Received Per Idea

Idea A -- Reentry Reminders

The feedback was the most direct on this one: "The stakes are the whole story. Everything else is implementation." My partner's only real pushback was on pricing and on whether a grant-funded org actually has the budget to say yes. His read was that $900 one-time is probably fine if there is a real warm contact and the person in the room can make the call without a 6-month procurement process. He also said the lived credibility is not a soft advantage -- "you are not pitching this as an outsider who read about reentry, you are pitching it as someone who came through it." That landed. Keep.

Idea B -- Scan-Op Admin

His feedback: technically interesting, lucrative market, but "do you actually know someone who does this?" When I said I do point-cloud processing but I do not have a warm contact with a solo operator actively complaining about admin, he said, "Then you're pitching a stranger, and that's a different exercise." He is right. The path to Idea B is a few genuine conversations with operators first -- not this week. Park.

Idea C -- Barbershop Booking

He had already found Booksy before I did. His take: "This is not a gap, it's a solved problem with a freemium tier. If you built this you'd be competing with free." Nothing to add to that. Drop.

Why Idea A is the strongest

The single most important thing the stress-test confirmed is that the warm contact plus lived experience is not sentimental -- it is a competitive advantage. The technical problem is not harder than what I built in Phase 2. The gap in the market is real and specific: nobody cheap does AI-structured intake plus automated SMS reminders for a tiny reentry program at a church-basement budget. The client already pays the cost in time and burnout. And I am not walking in as a stranger who saw a business opportunity. I am walking in as someone who knows what it feels like when a date gets dropped. That matters in a trust-first sale.

Deliverable

Three ideas through the four-question stress-test with partner feedback documented per idea. One clear drop (Idea C), one park with a clear on-ramp back (Idea B), one keeper (Idea A). Idea A named as strongest based on the intersection of real stakes, warm contact, lived credibility, and a genuine market gap. Taking it into competitor research and pricing in Exercise 3.
