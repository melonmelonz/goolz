The Backlog Becomes A Roadmap + Next Chapter

Exercise 5 / 5 . Level 2 . 30 min

Goal: take the Won't-Do list -- the features I cut to make the sprint fit -- and reframe it as a roadmap, prioritized for what I would build first if ReEntry Tech kept going, with an honest note on whether it should. And then close the phase pointed forward, not with a bow. The Won't-Do column was never a graveyard. It was a set of decisions with a date on them, and the date was the gate, and the gate is now behind me. So the same items that meant "not now" on Day 1 get to be reconsidered fresh, with the four-day clock gone.

Here is everything I cut, re-ordered by what I would actually build first now -- which is not the order I cut them in, because what matters least under a deadline is not what matters least once the product is real and a person is using it.

The Honest Question -- Should It Keep Going At All

I have to ask this and not flinch from it. ReEntry Tech works and one person uses it, and the most useful thing I could do for him is maybe not to keep adding features -- it is to make sure the thing he relies on stays up and stays simple. There is a real version of the future where the right move is not "build the roadmap" but "hand it off" -- to an org that does reentry work full time and can own it past my attention span, or to keep it small and stable for exactly one person and call that enough. A roadmap is not a promise to build everything on it. It is what I would build if continuing is the right call, and I am genuinely not sure it is. That uncertainty is honest and I am leaving it in.

"Won't Do this sprint" was never "never" -- it was a dated decision, and the date has passed

This is the reframe I want to keep. On Day 1, cutting the second AI feature felt a little like a loss -- the thing I most wanted to build, gone to a column called Won't Do. But it was never "never." It was "not before the gate," a decision with a deadline attached, and now the deadline is behind me and the item is free to be reconsidered on its merits. The discipline of the cut and the freedom of the roadmap are the same act seen from two sides of a date. Nothing in that column died. It was just waiting for now.

What this phase taught, and where the next one points

The phase taught me the thing I came in weakest at: that shipping is mostly deciding. Seven weeks proving I could write the code, one week proving I could decide what not to write -- which estimate to trust, which feature to cut, when to call a thing done and walk away while it is still imperfect. Done is a decision, not a feeling; cutting is a skill, not a defeat; estimates run long, so plan for it. Those are not coding lessons. They are the lessons that turn someone who can build into someone who can finish, and I have the empty plan with every box checked to prove I finished.

Where it points next: there is a phase after this one, and there is a bigger thing past that -- the capstone I keep circling, the recursive-acronym project that has been sitting at the edge of my attention while I built this. ReEntry Tech was a phase, not the destination. It proved I can take an idea, scope it down to a thing one real person uses, and carry it across a gate. That is exactly the muscle the next thing needs, at a bigger size. I am not closing a book today. I am turning a page, and the next chapter is already half-legible in the margin.

Checkpoint -- Phase 2, Done

Gate passed. App live, a real person on it daily. Sprint retro filed with one calibrating lesson underlined. The Won't-Do list reframed into a prioritized roadmap, with the honest question about whether to keep going left honestly open. The plan from Day 1 has a checkmark on every line and the growing untouched list it replaced does not exist anymore. That is the whole phase, and it is finished -- not perfectly, but decisively, which I have learned this week is the only way things actually finish.

Deliverable

The Won't-Do list reframed as a five-item roadmap, prioritized for what I'd build first if ReEntry Tech continues -- led by the categories UI the panel flagged and the second AI feature I most wanted -- with an honest, unresolved note on whether to continue at all or hand it off. The phase closed on forward motion: what shipping taught about deciding, and a light gesture at the capstone waiting next. Phase 2, done.
