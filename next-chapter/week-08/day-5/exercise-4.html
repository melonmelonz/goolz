Sprint Retrospective

Exercise 4 / 5 . Level 2 . 35 min

Goal: a blameless, specific retrospective of the whole sprint that produces at least one real change for next time -- not a feelings session, a calibration. Three columns: what went well, what went badly, what I would do differently. The rule is blameless, which is easy when the team is one person and also easy to fake by being soft on myself, so the discipline here is to be as specific about my own mistakes as I would be about a teammate's. The most useful output is not the win list. It is the estimate I got wrong, written down with the real number.

WhatThe evidence
P1s killed first, Day 1both AI-summary P1s dead on the first afternoon, plus a P2 -- the scariest items handled exactly where the plan put them
The MoSCoW cut held all weekthe second AI feature stayed cut from Monday to Friday; I refused it every time and a must-have got finished instead

What went badly

WhatThe honest detail

What I'd do differently

ChangeWhy

Build the empty state on Day 1, not Day 2it was the first screen my user would see and I scheduled it second -- the first impression should be the first build

Hold the commit rhythm even when tiredthe lump commit was the one night a clean history nearly became a mess
The Retro, In Numbers

Tied to the real week so it is not vibes: 5 things worked, 4 things hurt, 4 changes for next time. Two P1s dead on Day 1, one feature cut and held for five days, one estimate off by roughly 2x, one should-have left undone, three real users by Day 3, one dry run under time. The shape of the week is a strong plan executed with a couple of honest misses -- which is about the best a first real sprint can look.

The most useful line is the estimate I got wrong

Everything else in this retro is nice to know. The win list tells me what to keep doing, which I would mostly have kept doing anyway. The one line that actually changes the next sprint is "empty state, called small, ran 2x," because the next time I write "small" next to a feature with an empty and a loading and an error state, I will remember this one and write "medium." That is the whole point of a retro -- not to feel anything about the week, but to make the next estimate less wrong. The bad estimate is the only line I can spend, so it is the one I underline.

Deliverable

A completed blameless retro: three columns tied to the real numbers, five wins, four misses named specifically, four concrete changes for next time -- with the 2x empty-state estimate underlined as the one line that calibrates the next sprint.
