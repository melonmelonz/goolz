Final Pre-Flight

Exercise 1 / 5 . Level 1 . 20 min

Goal: prove the live thing works before I stand in front of anyone, by opening it cold and clicking it -- not trusting my memory of it working three days ago. The app has been frozen since Day 3, but "frozen" is a thing I believe and pre-flight is a thing I check. This is the twenty minutes that prevents the demo-day heart attack: the headline feature 500ing in front of the room because something shifted and I never reloaded. I open the production URL on my phone, the way the panel will open it, and I walk the four criteria with a piece of evidence beside each line.

Open it cold, on the real network

The single most common pre-flight miss is testing localhost and calling the live URL checked. They are different machines with different environment variables -- the whole Day 1 P1 was an env var that existed locally and not on Vercel. So I open the production URL specifically, on cellular specifically, signed out of anything, the way a stranger would. If it works there, it works.

Confirm the demo materials are loaded

Pre-flight is not only the app. It is the kit I present with. A working app and a fumbled scramble for the right browser tab still reads as unprepared. So the second half of the check is the materials, laid out in the order I'll need them.

The five-minute check that prevents the heart attack

If I only had five minutes, it is these three clicks: add one opportunity and watch it hit the board, run one AI summary and watch the bullets come back, and load the URL on my phone off cellular. Create-read works, the AI feature works on the deployed build, and it is genuinely live on a real network. Everything else in the kit is presentation polish. Those three clicks are the difference between "it works" being a belief and being a thing I watched happen ninety seconds before I started talking.

What I found

Nothing broken, which is the boring outcome you want from a pre-flight. One small thing: the AI summary took closer to four seconds than three on the cold load, the first call after the serverless function spun up. Not a bug -- a cold start -- but worth knowing so I narrate it in the demo ("first one's a little slow, it's waking up") instead of standing there in dead air looking like the thing hung. Pre-flight does not only catch failures. It catches the things I should say out loud so a normal delay does not read as a crash.

Deliverable

A completed pre-flight: four criteria clicked and confirmed on the live production URL over a real network, the full demo kit loaded in order, and one cold-start quirk noted to narrate rather than panic over. The app is ready and so is the kit. Next exercise maps each gate requirement to the exact artifact, so "show me where" is never a scramble.
