Audit My App

Exercise 1 / 5 . Level 1 . 30 min

Goal: a complete, honest picture of where ReEntry Tech actually stands. Not where I remember it standing, not where the demo-day version in my head stands -- where the real thing stands when I click every button. I opened the app in one window and the code in the other and I went through it like a stranger who is annoyed at me, writing down everything. Three columns: Works, Broken, Missing. The instruction was to be brutal, and the instruction was right -- better I find these now than my user finds them on a live URL, or worse, than I find them mid-presentation on Day 4.

The bug my head was protecting

I knew the AI summary was shaky on deploy. I had known it for days, the way you know a noise in your car. Writing "500 on the deployed app" into a column literally titled Broken is what made it real -- and made it a P1 instead of a someday. The audit's whole job is to drag the thing you have been not-looking-at into a row with a priority next to it.

Why "deployed" is in Missing even though it half-exists

I have a Vercel project and a build that succeeds, so it is tempting to call deploy "done." But the deployed thing crashes on its headline feature, so functionally a real person cannot use the real app. Until the live URL works end-to-end for someone who is not me, deploy stays unfinished -- and it is going to anchor the must-have list, not the works column.

Deliverable

A complete audit: five things that work, eight bugs each with a priority, four missing pieces each with an effort estimate. Two P1s, both clustered on the AI feature and both about the gap between localhost and a live URL -- which is exactly the gap I said in standup I was anxious about, now written down as rows instead of a feeling. Next exercise folds all of this into one backlog and sorts it.
