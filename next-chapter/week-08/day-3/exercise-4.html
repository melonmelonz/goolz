Documented User Activity

Exercise 4 / 5 . Level 2 . 45 min

Goal: the last Must, and the one I cannot do alone. Get the real user -- the guy who came home in March -- onto the live URL, watch him use it without coaching, then two more people, and capture a screenshot plus one sentence of feedback per person. This is the part I had been avoiding, because it is the part where the thing leaves my control. I split it deliberately into two phases: use it, then capture it. If I skip the capture I will remember it went well and walk into the gate with nothing.

Phase one -- use it (and shut up)

I texted my user the link. The OG card unfurled with the name and the pitch, he tapped it, and the app loaded on his phone -- a phone that is not mine, on a cell network, over HTTPS. Then the hardest instruction of the whole day: do not narrate. I had to sit on my hands and let him figure it out. The whole value is in watching the confusion happen, not in smoothing it over.

And it happened immediately. He tapped the whole opportunity card to open it -- the way you tap anything on a phone -- and nothing happened, because the edit affordance was a small button in the corner he never reached for. He paused, a little confused, and that pause was worth more than any test I wrote. He was not wrong. The card looks tappable, so it should be tappable. I built the interaction the way it was easy to build, not the way a thumb expects it to behave. That is a real usability finding, and there is no way I reason my way to it from my own keyboard.

What I watched, without coaching

User 1 (the real one): tapped the whole card to open it, not the small edit button -- reached for the obvious affordance and it was not wired. Then added a real opportunity (his forklift cert, deadline Jul 14) and saw it land on the board. Pasted one of his two job leads into the AI summary and read the bullets out loud.

User 2 and 3: a classmate and my sister, neither of whom I briefed. Both found create and filter without help. One tried to search before realizing the chips were filters; one also went for the card tap, which makes the finding two-for-three -- it is not just my guy, it is how people expect cards to behave.

Phase two -- capture it (before it evaporates)

The discipline: capture the evidence in the moment, every time, before I let myself feel good about it. A screenshot of each person's session and one sentence of feedback in their words. I logged each one as I went so nothing depended on my memory of a good afternoon.

"I think it works" vs "three people used it, here are the screenshots"

Those are not the same sentence and only one of them survives the gate. For a week I had a feeling the app worked. Now I have three screenshots of three people using it on the live URL and three sentences in their words. The feeling was never evidence. The discipline of capturing it -- in the moment, before the afternoon's good mood turned it into a vague memory -- is the only thing that converts "it went well" into something I can put in front of someone who was not there.

What the watching changed

The card-tap finding is going straight onto the list -- making the whole card open the opportunity is a small change and it is clearly the right one, because two of three people reached for it. I would never have found it by myself. That is the entire argument for getting a real user on the thing before the gate instead of after: the most important bug of the day was not in my code, it was in the gap between how I built the interaction and how a person expected it to work, and only a person could show me where that gap was.

Checkpoint

The gate Must is done with evidence behind it: three real people on the live URL, three screenshots, three feedback sentences, and a real usability finding I could not have reached alone. Plus cookieless analytics quietly confirming the opens actually happened. The scariest item on the whole week's list, handled -- and it turned out the terror was the point, because the thing I was afraid of watching is exactly the thing that taught me the most.
