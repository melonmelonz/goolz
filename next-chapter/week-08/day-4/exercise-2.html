The CRUD Demo Script

Exercise 2 / 5 . Level 2 . 35 min

Goal: a click-path I can walk in my sleep that shows Create and Read working end-to-end, cleanly, without my fate resting on typing a form correctly in front of a room. The gate wants to see the app do its core loop. The mistake is treating that as improv -- opening the live app cold and hoping. I have watched solid projects stall because someone fat-fingered a form on stage and burned ninety seconds recovering. This exercise is scripting the loop and rehearsing it so my hands run the path while my mouth does the talking.

Pre-seed so the read step has something real

The board should not be empty when I open it. An empty board is true to my user's first-run, but in a demo it makes the Read step look like nothing happened. So I seed one sensible record before the gate -- a real-shaped Opportunity that gives the board something to show and gives me a known thing to point at. The forklift cert is the obvious pick, because it is the record from the real story: a deadline of Jul 14, a type of cert, an org. It is concrete and it is the one I have been talking about all week. 

// prisma/seed.js -- one known record so the demo never opens to an empty board
await prisma.opportunity.create({
  data: {
    title: "Forklift certification -- deadline Jul 14",
    type: "cert",
    org: "Regional Workforce Center",
    location: "downtown",
    deadline: new Date("2026-07-14"),
    notes: "OSHA forklift cert, free for first-90-days program.",
    category: { connect: { name: "Training" } },
  },
});

The script -- a line at every click

The path is Read, then Create, then Read again so the new row shows up. At each click I have one rehearsed sentence. Not a paragraph -- a sentence -- because the point is to keep moving and let the app do the talking.

ClickWhat I say
Open the live URL"This is the board -- every opportunity my user is tracking, newest first. Here's the forklift cert with its Jul 14 deadline."
Tap "Add""Adding one is the core loop -- title, type, an optional org and deadline."
Type the known record"Say he gets a warehouse lead -- title, type job, the org, paste the link." (a short record I've typed twenty times)
Submit"It validates the title isn't blank, writes to Postgres through Prisma, and revalidates the board."
Land back on the board"And there it is, top of the list -- that's Create and Read end-to-end on the live URL."
// the create record I rehearse -- short, all-known, no surprises
title:  "Warehouse picker -- 2nd shift"
type:   "job"
org:    "Eastside Logistics"
source: "https://example-jobs.com/warehouse-picker"
// (deadline + notes left blank on purpose -- fewer fields to fat-finger)
A scripted demo is not dishonest

I had a hangup that scripting the demo was somehow cheating -- like a real demo should be improvised to prove the app really works. That's backwards. Improvising in front of a room wastes the room's time on my typing, and a fumble teaches them nothing about the app. Knowing exactly what I'll click and say is respecting the audience's time. The app's honesty is in it being live and real; my honesty is in showing it at its clearest, not at its most accidental.

The recovery line, decided in advance

Sometimes it breaks anyway -- the network stalls, a deploy is mid-flight, the OpenAI call is slow. The rule is to decide the recovery line now, in calm, so I am not improvising composure under stress. One sentence, then move on, no apologizing into the void. 

If the create hangs or errors live:
  -> "That's the network being slow -- here's the same flow on the
      record I added earlier," and I point at the seeded forklift cert.
  Then keep moving. No narrating the failure, no debugging on stage.
Why a smooth recovery reads as confidence

A demo that hiccups and recovers cleanly actually lands as more confident than one that never hiccups, because it shows I know the app well enough to route around a problem instead of freezing on it. The pre-seeded record is the safety net -- whatever fails live, I always have a real row already on the board to fall back to. The recovery line turns a potential disaster into a two-second pivot.

Checkpoint

A rehearsed Create-then-Read click-path with a one-line script at each step, a short pre-typed create record chosen to minimize fumble, one seeded forklift-cert row so the board is never empty and the read always shows something real, and a single recovery line decided in advance with the seeded record as the fallback. Walked end-to-end three times until my hands knew it. The demo no longer depends on live-typing luck -- which is the whole point.
